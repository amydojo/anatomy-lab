BODY / LIVE · V5.4 NERVE TRUTH

Sources, not spectacle.

Eleven sourced 3D skeletal and muscle meshes come from BodyParts3D 4.0. An independently sourced Z-Anatomy nerve surface is registered to the hip as an unreviewed mixed-reference composite. Its traced centreline, hip pivot, axes of motion and muscle action remain approximate and not clinically validated. This is not a diagnostic or therapeutic tool.

BodyParts3D © The Database Center for Life Science (DBCLS), Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. What actually moves

The source geometry is preserved in the reference pose. The femur is rotated rigidly around an estimated, fixed hip centre, with independent educational controls for flexion, abduction and axial rotation. The pelvis and sacrum remain fixed; the muscle meshes remain in their source pose. During femur movement, muscles are dimmed to avoid implying that the source meshes represent accurate dynamic contraction or attachment tracking.

No validated kinematic centre, joint-contact force, ligament stress, tendon excursion, capsule mechanics, or muscle moment arm is measured. The three axes are simplified, not clinical range-of-motion guidance. The movement labels express broad biomechanical categories rather than individual anatomical predictions.

Sciatic anatomy boundary

The golden pathway traces an estimated surface centreline when Z-Anatomy geometry is present, otherwise it uses a clearly labeled schematic fallback. The sciatic nerve generally leaves the pelvis below piriformis and courses through the posterior thigh, but variations occur. This route does not map an individual's nerves, establish entrapment, or explain symptoms. Sensory territories overlap and numbness distributions are not diagnostic on their own.

The original deep hip lab

The existing Hip / Exposed remains available as an SVG dissection view. Hip / Spatial adds sourced surfaces and 3D camera movement, but does not erase or replace existing lesson content.

Reproducible import and review

Importer: scripts/import_bp3d_hip.py. Source JSON: hip-source.json. The 4.0-derived hip bones and muscles use one reference frame; the separate Z-Anatomy nerve uses a measured femur registration to create a mixed-source composite. The resulting scene still requires independent anatomical review.

Pre-release review must inspect: pelvic orientation, femoral-head location, acetabular relationship, piriformis and deep rotator spatial order, gluteal depth, nerve route, possible misleading overlaps during movement, and rendered anterior/posterior labels. Automated geometry and browser tests do not satisfy this review.

V5.4 — Source-verified nerve surface, not a clinical nerve reconstruction

The right sciatic, tibial and common fibular nerve meshes come from Z-Anatomy, as exported and Draco-optimized in nqwrc/3d-anatomy. These are anatomical atlas surfaces created from source curves, not nerve masks from medical imaging.

The numerical fit measures femur surface agreement, NOT nerve-placement validity, and was not reviewed by a radiologist or anatomist. Graph and trace: source hierarchy identifies a sciatic trunk with tibial and common fibular continuations. The gold line is a visual centerline derived from 3D surface slices, not an observed path from diffusion MRI or micro-CT. No clinical segmentation accuracy is claimed. See isolated sourced geometry and graph.

Imaging dataset audit: The Pennsieve dataset 692 (CC BY 4.0) provides high-resolution imaging, microCT segmentations and fascicle connectivity data from six ~9 cm excised sciatic nerve segments from one human donor. It is valuable for a future Inside the Nerve feature but does not contain a whole sciatic nerve in situ inside this pelvis. It was not imported or aligned in V5.4. Open-access nerve-root MRI datasets likewise do not independently verify the pictured sciatic trunk in the pelvis.

Expert review remains pending: require anatomist/radiologist assessment of laterality, proximal sciatic course relative to piriformis, bone and muscle reference alignment, centerline continuity and branching, anatomical variation and source license obligations before any clinical-grade or medical-accuracy claim.
