VOL. 06 · PROVENANCE / VALIDITY

What is sourced. What is inferred.

Educational reference atlas, not a medical-grade or patient-specific reconstruction. Qualified anatomical and licensing reviews of the integrated Hip, Shoulder and Nerve work are pending. A linked scene or smooth animation cannot establish biological continuity.

01 / Full-body overview

The overview uses 78 pinned BodyParts3D 4.0 skeletal reference-pose components. It is derived from the pinned atlas with one uniform transform. Simplified mobile assets retain per-part FMA IDs, source hashes and reduction counts in the provenance manifest. BodyParts3D © the Database Center for Life Science, CC BY 4.0. Missing hands, feet, soft tissues and organs mean this is not a complete anatomical body.

02 / Hip and knee

Hip and Knee use source-matched BodyParts3D 4.0 geometry. The verified hip-knee frame transform aligns 480 corresponding femoral vertices within rounding. This proves a skeletal reference correspondence, NOT clinical biomechanics or nerve placement. In V6.9, the eight existing source-derived hip muscles follow source pelvis/sacrum-to-femur endpoints using unreviewed proximity-derived attachment gradients. The knee now imports five additional real right muscle surfaces from the same pinned BodyParts3D release: popliteus, vastus medialis, lateralis and intermedius, and the short head of biceps femoris. See their individual source identities, hashes and limitations and geometry. They bend toward the moving tibia, fibula or patella in the original WebGL scene. This is approximate teaching motion, not verified insertion anatomy, quadriceps/patellar tendon tracking, volume-preserving muscle mechanics, or patient-specific joint kinematics. Neither the menisci, ligaments, other muscles nor sciatic continuity is invented.

03 / Right shoulder and rotator cuff

The new in-place shoulder lab uses the same pinned BodyParts3D 4.0 reference frame as the skeletal overview: three right-sided source bones (scapula, clavicle and humerus) and real source meshes for supraspinatus, infraspinatus, subscapularis and teres minor. Additional source muscles include teres major, portions of deltoid, biceps brachii, coracobrachialis and clavicular pectoralis major; consult the per-structure shoulder manifest and geometry for the exact available meshes and their hashes. The middle/acromial deltoid is not represented as verified source here. No fabricated labrum, capsule, tendon, glenohumeral ligament, axillary nerve or brachial plexus geometry is claimed. The V6.8 arm-abduction control drives a connected, estimated three-bone kinematic model: the clavicle elevates around a source-derived approximate sternoclavicular pivot, the scapula rotates/tilts around a paired acromioclavicular point, and the humerus follows the moving estimated glenohumeral center. Smooth, bounded movement curves are deliberately not a rigid 2:1 or 3:1 scapulohumeral rule; a 2025 systematic review documents large variability in normal scapular motion. Muscle origins follow the clavicle or scapula, while inferred humeral insertions follow the humerus; the mesh between them uses bounded skinning weights. The Show rig points control displays estimated SC/AC/GH pivots and selected rotator-cuff attachment paths in the shared camera. Source geometry is unchanged. Pivots, attachment landmarks, curves, wrapping paths, muscle volume, bone collisions, and scapulohumeral rhythm have NOT been anatomist-reviewed, measured, or biomechanically validated. This is educational visual motion only.

04 / The sciatic composite

The Z-Anatomy-derived sciatic, tibial and common-fibular surfaces are separate from the BodyParts3D hip, with CC BY-SA 4.0 derivative license notice. They were registered by automatic femoral ICP; the nerve manifest documents exact inputs, transforms, errors and limitations. Median ~6.2 mm and P95 ~14.3 mm describe femur surface fit, not independently measured sciatic nerve location errors. Shared donor is not verified. The sampled nerve path and branch gaps are estimates, not imaged fascicle or MR neurography reconstructions.

05 / Explicit source seam

The Hip → Knee transition shows a source-boundary message. The Knee model does not contain a continuously reconstructed sciatic nerve. We do not draw imaginary connective nerve tissue through unavailable anatomy.

06 / Whole-body atlas expansion (V7.0)

Six more regions are sourced from exactly the same pinned BodyParts3D 4.0 reference: skull and face (14 bones), cervical neck (8), ribs and thorax (30), elbow (3), wrist (15), ankle/foot (14). This is 84 source-mesh appearances, with shared bones deliberately reused between regions rather than duplicated as different anatomy. All source parts are attributed by FMA identity and checked by SHA-256 in the full expansion provenance manifest. Each region retains its original full-resolution imported mesh; the 78-mesh overview remains a separate source-backed level of detail. Camera navigation stays in one 3D renderer and shared reference frame, not an external iframe.

V7.1 licensed muscle expansion. The six detail regions now include 73 additional named source muscle meshes from the same pinned BodyParts3D 4.0 release: 16 in skull/eye and jaw-adjacent anatomy, 14 cervical, 11 thoracic, 10 elbow, 10 wrist, and 12 lower-leg/foot. Select Muscles in a region to show the muscles with subdued skeletal context; tap a visible structure to inspect its name. The original, source-model FMA concept IDs, raw upstream classifications, exact source chunk hashes and every region asset hash are recorded in the updated provenance manifest. A few named lower-leg muscles are categorized under skeletal by the upstream data; that upstream label is preserved, rather than silently altered.

Coverage is a licensed subset, not every muscle in the human body. In particular, facial muscles of expression and major jaw-closing muscles absent from the pinned model are not manufactured or mislabeled. Some foot/hand deep muscles, connective tissues and specialized joint mechanics remain unrepresented. Where elbow/wrist/ankle controls are available, skinning is estimated by source-bone surface proximity and is not an anatomist-reviewed origin/insertion map, validated tendon path, accurate muscle force, physiological contraction, or clinical animation. V8.2 adds separately labeled, source-registered teaching transforms for mandible opening, distributed cervical movement, and small rib lifts; these are unvalidated motion demonstrations, not physiological jaw, neck or breathing simulation.

Source anatomy is not clinical simulation. The elbow, wrist and ankle controls use estimated primary hinges plus a separately bounded regional secondary axis; their rotation centers are derived from source-bone bounding regions, not peer-reviewed measured motion. These demonstrations do not reconstruct ligament constraints, carpal or tarsal coupling, cartilage, soft-tissue collision, or tendon mechanics. V8.2 adds bounded educational jaw opening, segment-weighted cervical rotation and bilateral rib elevation. Neither temporomandibular sliding, physiological coupled spine motion, nor clinically realistic ventilation is claimed. Skeletal surfaces alone do not imply anatomical or biomechanical review.

07 / V8 canonical whole-body specimen

The home specimen is now a source-aligned, independently hashed, deduplicated overview. It reuses the original 78 simplified skeletal elements and adds actual BodyParts3D bone and muscle surfaces from the six verified detail atlas regions. It then imports source-matched left wrist/hand and left ankle/foot geometry, plus left upper/lower limb muscles, from pinned binary upstream chunks: V8 provenance and digest and overview LOD geometry. All parts retain FMA identity, source release coordinates and the original licensing. The imported right-sided detail labs do not automatically become bilateral; left-sided materials are limited to the shared overview.

Rendering and validation boundaries: Bones, muscles and combined tissue views show selected source meshes at simplified resolution. They do not represent complete anatomy, physiological muscle loading, MRI-derived shape, ligament mechanics, articular cartilage, mapped nerve continuity, or reviewed attachment points. The feet and hands remain incomplete (including phalanges), and facial movement muscles are not fully present. Bilateral source context should not be mistaken for symmetric clinical motion. GPU cleanup, WebGL fallback, SHA checks and mobile browser tests establish software quality, not anatomical validation. Region-by-region expert review is required before any medical use.

08 / V8.2 regional teaching motion

Each source-backed region now has a bounded named primary movement: right hip flexion, right knee flexion, right shoulder elevation, mandibular opening, segment-weighted cervical flexion, small bilateral rib elevation, right elbow flexion, right wrist flexion and right ankle dorsiflexion. Secondary teaching axes are available for hip abduction/adduction, tibial rotation, humeral internal/external rotation, cervical rotation, forearm pronation/supination, wrist radial/ulnar deviation, and subtalar inversion/eversion. Neutral restores exactly the original atlas reference-pose geometry; slider constraints prevent undefined motions. The sciatic nerve view remains intentionally static because no validated source exists for a continuous moving nerve pathway.

All V8.2 rotations, approximate bone-surface pivot estimates, muscle attachment gradients and range limits are illustrative engineering conventions, not anatomist-approved joint kinematics or patient-specific anatomy. The source body meshes are not changed, torn or manufactured. Intercostal muscles and the diaphragm remain in their pinned reference pose when a rib-lift demonstration is played: this cannot be read as a complete respiration simulation. No cartilage wrapping, complex joint coupling, tendon slack, tissue volume preservation, collision contact, realistic contraction, ligament forces or quantitative range-of-motion inference is validated. The evidence and scientific review gates remain open.

V10 / Cross-version facial and mastication muscles

The 47 facial-expression and chewing-muscle surfaces now included in the optional facial overlay originate from BodyParts3D 3.0 (2011) via the OMFAtlas registered open atlas. These are genuinely sourced facial meshes, not procedurally drawn muscles. They are stored as a separate adapted dataset under CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan; the earlier 4.0 atlas geometry retains its own CC BY 4.0 terms. License and attribution: BodyParts3D, Copyright© The Database Center for Life Science licensed by CC Attribution-Share Alike 2.1 Japan.

The original 3.0 OBJs were aligned by OMFAtlas to the 4.0 head using shared mandible, frontal and maxilla controls. Its fourth held-out left-maxilla check reports 0.258 mm source-to-target RMS and 0.089 mm reverse RMS. BODY / LIVE verifies the pinned upstream Git blobs, per-part original source SHA-256 records, transformed mesh topology and a local head-bound/mandible proximity gate; the upstream fit has not independently been recomputed from original bone controls here. Display coordinates are converted from OMFAtlas registered metres to BODY / LIVE's [x,(y−0.9),z]×5 frame.

These facial muscles are static source-pose references. No new facial-expression, temporomandibular sliding, chewing-force, tendon-attachment or clinically validated jaw movement is implied. The source import and checks are described in V10 source manifest and the accompanying validation record. An anatomist's independent review remains pending. The coccyx and six middle-ear ossicles are still not included: 199 of the conventional 206 bones, rather than a false 206-bone claim.
